The Nuclear Fuel Cycle

A GIS story that follows nuclear fuel from uranium extraction to conversion, enrichment, fabrication, reactor use, spent-fuel management and geological disposal. Instead of treating a nuclear power plant as an isolated point, the map reveals the wider industrial geography that makes nuclear energy possible. Each stage is shown separately, so the role of mines, chemical plants, enrichment facilities, fuel factories, reactors, storage centres and repositories can be read without visual overlap.

Each step shows one functional layer at a time. The previously displayed nuclear layer is hidden before the next one appears, so the geography of each stage remains easy to read. Use the atlas controls in the side bar to prepare or restore the wider map whenever you want. By the final step, the map shows the complete chain.

The nuclear system as geography

A reactor is only one node

Nuclear energy is often represented by the power station alone. In reality, a reactor depends on a long chain of mines, chemical plants, enrichment facilities, fuel factories, transport routes, storage centres, research laboratories and waste repositories.

Use the steps above to build that geography progressively on the map.
